Why SEO Matters

It’s not a hard sell: search results matter in a web-based industry. I'm not an expert in search engine optimization — and that’s fine. Keep reading.

This article goes beyond what the title might suggest. “How to Write the <head> Section of Your Webpage" didn't quite have the same ring. And honestly, there are better resources out there for that sort of thing.

What follows are a few nuggets of what seemed important in the world of SEO — and the extraordinary lengths I was willing to go to make the simple task of writing 10–15 lines of HTML much, much harder.

The Spec

I saw an ad for a gig-work platform just days before diving into this project. “You’re a business owner — you’re not going to hire an SEO expert. You’re going to spend days learning to do it yourself.”

All I can tell you is that you need endpoints for robots.txt and sitemap.xml, plus metadata for Facebook’s and Twitter’s protocols — the ones that generate rich previews. And maybe throw in some JSON-LD so machine that learn can connect tangents in the same ontology or whatever.

Sounds easy, right? I agree. But it doesn't scale, and it’s too prone to human error. I’m a human — and I wouldn't trust me to put letters in the right order to form a sentence without spell-check. So forget about consistently compiling SEO best practices by hand.

Instead, I built a system for visibility and auditing — one I’d trust to scale to 50 or 500 articles.

CMS Continued

So, it’s clear at this point that I need a form. Or several. I need a format (JSON), and I need to store it somewhere (a file). And while I’m at it, I might as well add a public/private toggle with some basic whitelisting logic for routes. Just like that, I’ve got the beginnings of a publication pipeline — for the content management system I’m not supposed to be building.

Well, it’s not quite a CMS pipeline yet. Right now, article fragments are read from disk and injected into a Jinja2 template — all in the same route. In a proper CMS, those fragments (and the head tags) would be exposed through an API, letting the service supply multiple sites with different (or the same) articles.

That’s a simple enough task — and probably worth knocking out before it’s strictly necessary. On the opposite end of the spectrum: writing the serializer that converts HTML fragments into the JSON schema needed by the CMS’s rich text editor.

That part seems a lot harder. And I still need to figure out thumbnails, cascading fields, and overrides — before even thinking about touching the editor again. If ever.
